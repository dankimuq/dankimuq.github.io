A Little Longer bio

Dr. Dan Dongseong Kim is the Deputy Director of UQ Cybersecurity and an Associate Professor (in the Commonwealth system) with a continuing appointment in Cyber Security at The University of Queensland (UQ), Brisbane, Australia. In his current role, he is dedicated to advancing research and education in cybersecurity, focusing on the security and dependability of various systems and networks.

Before joining UQ, Dr. Kim was a faculty member at the University of Canterbury (UC) in Christchurch, New Zealand, from 2011 to 2018. During his tenure at UC, he served as a Senior Lecturer in Cyber Security from 2015 to 2018 and as a Lecturer from 2011 to 2014 in the Department of Computer Science and Software Engineering. His leadership in the cybersecurity field at UC was marked by significant contributions to both research and teaching.

Dr. Kim’s academic journey includes a postdoctoral position at Duke University in Durham, North Carolina, USA, from 2008 to 2011. Prior to that, he was a visiting scholar at the University of Maryland, College Park, Maryland, USA, in 2007. These experiences allowed him to collaborate with leading experts and broaden his research horizons.

His research interests lie in cybersecurity and dependability for various systems and networks. He has a particular focus on developing robust security mechanisms for critical infrastructure, IoT devices, and networked systems. His work aims to enhance the resilience and reliability of these systems against cyber threats, ensuring their safe and secure operation.

Dr. Kim has an impressive publication record, with over 185 publications and more than 7,000 citations. His Google Scholar profile shows an h-index of 41 and an i10-index of 117. Some of his notable research areas include:

  • Graphical Models for Cyber Security: Model-based cyber security risk analysis

  • AI for Cybersecurity & Cybersecurity for AI: Securing AI systems and applying AI techniques to cybersecurity

  • Moving Target Defense (MTD): Resilient and proactive defense

  • Evolving Attacks and Defense Automation: Red team and blue team automation and evaluation using AI

Dr. Kim is actively involved in professional activities, serving as an Associate Editor for IEEE Communications Surveys and Tutorials, and as an Editorial Board Member for Elsevier Computers & Security and Elsevier Computer Networks. He is also an elected member of IFIP WG 10.4 on Dependable Computing and Fault Tolerance and serves on several steering committees, including IFIP/IEEE DSN and IEEE PRDC.

Dr. Kim’s research has been supported by various sponsors, including the NSF (US), IBM T.J. Watson (US), US Army Research Laboratory/DEVCOM-Pacific (US), NEC (Japan), Tait Communications (NZ), MBIE (NZ), NPRP (Qatar), ADD (Republic of Korea), and NRF (Republic of Korea).

*Information for Prospective Students*

Please read carefully in order to be a member of my research groups.

0) If you are undergraduate students at UQ.

You are always welcome to come by my office (Building 78 Room 404) to chat with me :)

1) To be a Higher Degree Research (HDR) student.

- Before you could begin a Higher Degree Research (HDR) study here (as a master or Ph.D. student), you would need to be formally accepted as a Higher Degree Research (HDR) student at our university. Information about the enrollment procedure at our university, the academic and English language entry criteria, application forms, etc. can be found at: https://graduate-school.uq.edu.au/english-language-proficiency-requirements

Information about UQ's scholarships for the HDR students can be found at:

https://scholarships.uq.edu.au/

*Please note that I cannot provide any funding for you unless I have external funding.

2) To be an HDR student under my supervision

- Before making any statement about me being your supervisor, I first need to receive the following information:

Please MERGE the following documents into ONE Single PDF file and email to dan [dot] kim [at] uq [dot] edu [dot] au.

  1. Your C.V. (resume) in English

  2. English Language Test score (IELTS, TOEFL, etc.) - you should meet the minimum requirement of UQ. (English requirements)

  3. Academic transcript(s), CGPA (grade-point-average) and rank in your class/department; 

    • Example 1: GPA 3 out of 4.5 for Bachelor degree, 

    • Example 2: 6.3 out of 7 for Masters degree.

  4. Published (or accepted) research papers (in conferences/journals, technical reports, etc) if any.

  5. Funding plan (self, government, external, etc)

    • This should be clearly mentioned in your information. I do not usually fund HDR students and note that it is highly unlikely to get any UQ scholarships unless 1) your overall GPA (in Honours and/or Masters) is A+ (or close to A+) and 2) you have prior research experience and publications in refereed venues.

  6. Your research interest: please state it as specific as possible.

3) Paper review & Coding test

  1. Your background/research interest matches mine, you will be required to review a research paper. You will be given a research paper depending on topics of mutual interest. Please download the review form (here) and return your review within one week. 

  2. You may need to show your programming skill given coding request.

  3. You can have an interview only if you pass the two tests mentioned above.

The "Safelite" framework is a semi-automated, attacker-centric approach to software risk assessment, focusing on identifying and analyzing attack paths within a system. Interpretable, Dependable, and Secure Intrusion Detection System (IDS^2)

Interpretable (or eXplainable)

  • Supports both white-box and black-box models.

  • Provides explanations in natural language with clear boundary conditions and visualizations.

  • Enables justification, control, improvement, and discovery of security insights.

Dependable

  • Ensures safety and reliability against both accidental and intentional disruptions.

Secure

  • Designed to resist evasion attacks and maintain robustness against adversarial threats.

Intro to MTD

Research areas

ResearchAutonomous vehicles securityDeepfake detectionGraphical security modelsIntrusion detectionLLM & GenAI securityMoving target defenseIntroduction to MTDPapersRed & blue team automation

Moving Target Defense (MTD) is a proactive security approach that continuously shifts a system’s attack surface to increase uncertainty and complexity for attackers. By dynamically changing system configurations, network paths, or execution environments, MTD significantly reduces the effectiveness of reconnaissance and exploitation efforts. This paradigm enhances resilience by making systems less predictable and harder to compromise.
